My interest in technology started in 2018, when I built a small RFID project after purchasing my first kit. I spent time understanding how the signals worked, experimenting with hexadecimal codes and exploring what I could do with the technology. It was a fun experience that eventually led me to hacking courses, the Cardputer ADV and, later, a degree in Computer Science. Each of these experiences gave me another reason to keep exploring this field.

I am currently pursuing courses and further learning in **information security**. I have a strong interest in hacker culture: understanding how systems work, exploring their limits, researching vulnerabilities and learning how technology can be used in creative ways. I want to develop these skills through ethical hacking and responsible security practices.

At the same time, I want to build a professional career in **embedded systems**, with a particular focus on **firmware development**. The combination of programming and electronics is what makes this field especially interesting to me. I want to work on firmware for embedded devices, learning more about microcontrollers, low-level software, hardware interfaces, IoT and the security of connected devices.

Building a career in embedded systems and information security is not necessarily easy, especially in my region of Brazil, where opportunities and specialized communities can be limited. Sometimes I question whether the lack of progress comes from the local job market, limited resources or even my own lack of dedication. I have achieved good academic results and passed competitive selection processes, but I know that potential alone does not guarantee a successful career. For now, much of this exploration remains a learning process and a personal interest. One example is Netrunner, a firmware project I developed with the assistance of AI, which has given me an opportunity to experiment and learn.

Looking ahead, I intend to keep developing my knowledge in information security and embedded systems, and to pursue further studies in these areas. I do not know exactly where this path will take me, but my goal is clear: work with embedded firmware while continuing to explore information security and ethical hacking. I intend to keep learning, building projects and turning the curiosity that began in 2018 into a professional path.
