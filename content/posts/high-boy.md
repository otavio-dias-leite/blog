The **High Boy** is an interesting device for beginners exploring electronics and hardware hacking. I actually started with a Raspberry Pi Zero W. However, hacking isn't about running ready-made programs or pressing buttons; it's about understanding how things work, studying protocols, building solutions and, for example, creating your own server. Around R$700 for an ESP32 with a few extra components seems overpriced to me. Still, once it becomes available on AliExpress, I'll probably buy one as an enthusiast and a more experienced user.

What really interests me are the technical possibilities. During my Electrical Engineering studies, I learned about radio frequency (and, honestly, I didn't do very well in that subject, lol). I understand different approaches to building signal transmitters and receivers, and could even build my own radio station. In Computer Science, I also studied standardization and information security standards. It would be interesting to investigate which devices comply with these standards and exchange ideas with people working in the field.

I'd recommend the High Boy to curious beginners, but a tool doesn't replace knowledge. In the hands of someone without technical understanding, most of its features will have little practical value. For someone with criminal intentions, it isn't the magical hacking device they might imagine either. Turning TVs on and off using infrared is probably the most entertaining thing someone without the necessary knowledge will accomplish. The interesting part is understanding the technology, not simply pressing buttons.
