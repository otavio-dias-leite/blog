My interest in technology started long before university. As a child, I experimented with computers and game development: at 10, I was already building small games with Unity 3D and Amazon Lumberyard. At 12, my father and uncle gave me a Raspberry Pi and an Arduino, which led to years of hands-on experiments with electronics and physical devices. At 14, I entered a technical program in **Electrotechnics at IFF**, placing first in the entrance exam. At 17, I was admitted to **Electrical Engineering**; I did not check my exact ranking, but believe I was among the top four. At 20, I began a second degree in **Computer Science** at a private university. Along the way, I contributed to and helped consolidate formal projects, especially involving embedded systems and physical devices, and also carried out electrical installation work independently.

My main professional foundation is **Electrical Engineering and Electrotechnics**. I am looking for opportunities in **Industry 4.0**, particularly where electrical systems, embedded devices, and **IoT** meet — including autonomous systems and off-grid solar installations, monitoring, and control. Programming began as a hobby; studying Computer Science made me approach it more seriously. I completed my first semester with a GPA above **9.2**, while continuing to study and complete courses. My interests now also include **Information Security** and cloud computing. I am drawn to protecting networks and connected devices, not to breaking into systems. An early influence was experimenting with an Arduino RFID kit my father gave me, followed by building an infrared signal receiver and transmitter. Those projects helped spark my curiosity about access control, communications, and device security.

I have pursued cybersecurity bootcamps and training initiatives, including **Hackers do Bem**, and plan to develop my foundations further through study paths such as **CCNA, OSCP, and CEH**. I am also interested in training and certifications from providers such as **Solyd** and **DESEC** as complementary learning opportunities, rather than treating certificates as a substitute for practical knowledge. My goal is to build a career that connects electrical and physical systems with secure, connected infrastructure — especially embedded devices, industrial IoT, and network security. I am open to professional opportunities, internships, and collaborations in these areas.
