My background is in **electrical engineering and electronics, not software engineering**. At 14, I studied Electrotechnics; at 17, I began Electrical Engineering. My second degree is in **Computer Science**, not Information Systems.

My strongest foundation is understanding hardware and the physical principles behind it: electronic components, electrical systems, appliances, digital electronics, and computing infrastructure. I am also familiar with the broader foundations of computing, including networks, databases, and how systems fit together.

Programming has always been a hobby, and I have taken courses in cybersecurity, web development, and game development. But software development is not my main specialization, and I am not a code-producing machine. For complex software and front-end work, I often use AI-assisted development — what people call *vibe coding*. I also build smaller projects with high-level tools, engines, and sometimes natural-language workflows. I have studied C, C++, and digital electronics, but much of my programming practice remains driven by curiosity and personal projects.

The field I am actively working toward is **embedded systems**: firmware, microcontrollers, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, FreeRTOS, and the integration of hardware and software. My goal is to deepen this expertise, pursue postgraduate studies in Embedded Systems for IoT, and build practical projects across personal, professional, and independent contexts.

I am open to **jobs, internships, freelance work, and collaborations** related to embedded systems, electronics, firmware, and IoT. This blog documents that path and the projects I develop along the way.
